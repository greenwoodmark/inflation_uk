Bayesian updating when a CPI fit is rank deficient

A two-driver illustration of why today’s trades may update some combinations of month-on-month inflation growth while leaving other combinations anchored to yesterday’s estimate.

Two unknown drivers: a = 1st m/m inflation growth and b = 2nd m/m inflation growth.

Each trade does not necessarily observe a and b separately. It observes a weighted combination of them. If all today’s trades observe the same combination, the deterministic fit cannot uniquely recover both drivers.

trade target ≈ haa + hbb
Starting conditions: begin with the blue prior point and the default slider values. In the rank-deficient panel, the prior starts at (a,b)=(0.60,1.20), while today’s observed sum is a+b=3.60. The purple posterior must move onto that gray dashed line, but its split between a and b remains largely inherited from the prior. In the full-rank panel, the same separated prior is confronted with a+b=3.60 and a−b=−0.40, so both observations pull the posterior toward their unique intersection.
How to read the diagrams: a line is a set of driver values that is consistent with an observation. One line leaves many possible answers. Two independent lines intersect at one answer.

What the Bayesian update is doing

SituationWhat the trades identifyWhat the prior contributes
Rank deficientThe observed combination, such as a+b, updates both drivers through that combination.The allocation and uncertainty along the unobserved direction, such as a-b. With an independent equal-variance prior, the allocation is an equal split.
Full rankBoth independent combinations, such as a+b and a-b.Regularisation and uncertainty weighting, but not the basic uniqueness of the solution.

The important distinction

The rank-deficient Bayesian result is not a new deterministic fit to today’s data. It is a prior-informed posterior estimate: the likelihood updates both drivers through the observed a+b combination, while the prior supplies the allocation along the unobserved a−b direction. In this example, the independent equal-variance prior makes the update split equally between a and b, so a−b is preserved exactly. That is the simple two-driver analogue of the current six-driver shadow posterior.

For the USCPI application: a rank-deficient deterministic status remains rank-deficient. The Bayesian shadow curve is useful as an explicitly labelled exploratory estimate, but it should not be described as a fully identified canonical fit.
Calculation note. The example uses a Gaussian prior with independent variance 0.16 for each driver and observation variance 0.04. The posterior is calculated from the normal Bayesian linear update. With this symmetric prior and an a+b observation, both drivers receive equal posterior-mean updates and the posterior mean of a−b equals its prior mean. Values are illustrative, not USCPI market data.
