Jupiter / JUP EPS model

The performance-fee EPS model estimates Jupiter’s fee contribution by applying documented fund fee rates to positive returns above each fund’s benchmark and high-water-mark hurdle, multiplied by interpolated fund AUM. Inputs include annual-report AUM anchors, official NAV histories, KIID fee terms, benchmark assumptions, tax, variable compensation, overhead, and average shares outstanding.

A. Performance-fee EPS model: FY2024 and FY2025

Basic historical comparison using the three annual-report AUM anchors. GEAR is the material modelled fund. Jupiter UK Dynamic Long Short Equity is recognised as fee-bearing, but its current AUM is only £50.80m and no annual-report AUM anchor is available, so it is not included in the quantified estimate.

Important: The estimate is a fee-yield proxy, not an administrator HWM calculation. The KIID documents a 20% contractual fee for GEAR and reports an actual FY2025 fee equal to 2.76% of weighted-average share-class NAV. That 2.76% is used as the calibration yield for this deliberately simple comparison.
Coverage and fee-rate assumptions: The UCITS representative NAV is assumed to proxy additional GEAR offshore Cayman and segregated-fund performance-fee assets. The model adds 20% to the FY2024 fee base and 30% to the FY2025 and FY2026 fee bases. It assumes average performance-fee rates of 20% in FY2024, 19% in FY2025, and 18% in FY2026. These are sensitivity assumptions, not observed offshore AUM or verified group-wide fee rates.

Both scenarios use a 4% annual hurdle, an assumed 18% FY2026 average performance-fee rate, interpolated average AUM of £7.825bn, a 1.30 non-UCITS factor for assumed additional offshore Cayman and segregated-fund exposure, 45% staff/bonus drag, 2.5% overhead, 25% tax, and 526.8m shares.

The backtest uses GEAR’s representative NAV, a simple annual high-water mark, and factsheet-based annual hurdle rates of 5.3% for 2023 (applied to FY2024), 4.4% for FY2025, and 4.0% for FY2026. For each observation window, the applicable annual rate is compounded over actual calendar days; only closing NAV above that hurdle generates fee-bearing excess return. Excess return is multiplied by interpolated average fund AUM and the assumed period average performance-fee rate: 20% in FY2024, 19% for FY2025, and 18% for FY2026. To address public UCITS coverage blind spots, the fee base is uplifted by assumed additional offshore Cayman and segregated-fund exposure of 20%, 30%, and 30% respectively, with performance assumed similar to the UCITS vehicle. Estimated PF PBT applies 45% staff/bonus drag and 2.5% overhead; estimated PF PAT applies 25% tax, then divides by period-average shares. FY2024 combines the archived FE fundinfo representative series through 27 November 2024 with official Jupiter NAV thereafter. FY2026 scenarios extend current NAV and AUM to year-end 2026.

D. GEAR cumulative performance, drawdown, and assumed AUM

Cumulative performance is measured from the first available valuation on or after 1 January 2024. Running drawdown is measured from the running NAV peak. The AUM line uses the model’s annual/current anchors with linear interpolation and is shown on the right-hand axis. The chart is descriptive and does not reconstruct the contractual HWM or fee-accrual ledger.

F. Delta of JUP share price to GEAR performance

Each point compares the trailing 365-calendar-day GEAR NAV growth with the trailing 365-calendar-day JUP share-price return. The point date is the GEAR valuation date; each series uses the latest available observation on or before the current and prior dates. JUP return uses the unadjusted IBKR closing price, so it is a share-price return rather than a dividend-adjusted total return. The fitted line is an ordinary-least-squares regression of JUP return on GEAR growth; beta is its slope, measured in JUP percentage points per one percentage point of GEAR growth.
